The Codex

How to Play

Two-Headed Commander fuses the singleton deckbuilding and legendary commanders of EDH with the cooperative, simultaneous-turn structure of Two-Headed Giant.

I
Form Your Alliance

Two players form a team. Each player builds their own 100-card Commander deck, following standard Commander singleton rules — one copy of each card (except basic lands), built around a chosen legendary creature as their Commander.

✦ Each teammate selects their own Commander independently
II
Shared Life Total

Each team begins with a shared life total of 60, following Two-Headed Giant rules. All damage dealt to either player reduces this shared pool. Life gain by either teammate increases the shared total.

✦ Poison counters are also shared — 15 poison = team defeat
III
Simultaneous Turns

Both teammates take their turn simultaneously. The team shares a single combat phase — both players may attack and both draw during their draw step. Each player maintains their own mana pool and hand.

✦ Only the second team draw on their first turn
IV
Commander Rules Remain

All standard Commander rules apply: the commander tax (+2 per prior casting) applies per commander individually, and commander damage is tracked per-commander per player (21 combat damage from one commander eliminates that player's team).

✦ Color identity rules still apply to each deck separately
V
Winning & Losing

A team loses when their shared life total reaches 0, when either player receives 21 commander damage from a single commander, when either player accumulates 15 or more poison counters, or when either player must draw from an empty library. Teams win and lose together.

Rule Modifications

Key Differences

Two-Headed Commander is not simply stacking two formats together — specific rules from each parent format are modified, preserved, or replaced.

🛡
Shared Life: 60, Not 30

Standard 2HG uses 30 life per team. Here, teams start at 60 life to accommodate Commander's longer, more complex games and prevent aggro from ending games before commanders hit the table.

Modified from 2HG
👑
Four Commanders, Four Threats

Each player controls their own Commander. Opponents must track and respond to two separate commanders per team. Synergistic commander pairs — one threat, one support — are the heart of the format's strategy.

New to this format
⚡
No Mana Sharing

Players do not share mana pools. Each player taps their own lands and uses their own mana. This prevents degenerate pooling interactions while preserving individual deck identity.

Modified from 2HG
🤝
Open Communication

Teammates may freely discuss strategy, show each other their hands, and coordinate plays openly. There is no hidden information between allies — secrets are only kept from opponents.

Inherited from 2HG
☠
Infect & Poison

Poison counters are tracked per-team. At 15 poison counters (scaled from the standard 10) the team loses — adjusted to match the higher life total of this format.

Modified from 2HG
🎯
Targeting Restrictions

You cannot target your teammate with harmful spells but can target them with beneficial effects. "Each opponent" deals damage to each opposing player but only reduces the shared life total once per damage source.

Inherited from 2HG
Local Game Store

Why Run This Format?

Two-Headed Commander is designed to be LGS-friendly — exciting to spectate, fast to organise, and rewarding for players of all experience levels who want a team experience.

🎲
Built-in Socialising

Random team assignments every event mean players regularly partner with someone new. The 10-minute deck selection window becomes a high-energy moment of discovery — two strangers rapidly figuring out which of their decks synergise best.

⏱
Predictable Round Times

Hard 1-hour rounds with 3 extra turns then draw keep the event on schedule. Games rarely approach the time limit since both formats incentivise decisive play — teams that drag the game out risk the clock as much as opponents.

🃏
Two Decks Per Player

Each player registers two decks and chooses one after teams are announced. This rewards both collection breadth and format knowledge — knowing when to pivot from your go-wide deck to your combo deck based on your partner's list is a real skill.

🏆
Competitive with Heart

Three rounds of best-of-one keeps the night tight and the energy high. Because teams are randomised, strong players carry weaker ones and everyone contributes — reducing the gap between competitive and casual players in a way 1v1 events rarely achieve.

🔧
Curated for Fairness

The ban list specifically removes cards that create degenerate loops, lock games down indefinitely, or produce wins through attrition rather than board presence. Players are rewarded for building efficient, proactive decks — not prison strategies.

✨
Memorable Moments

Two commanders in play at once means twice the legendary synergy, twice the unexpected interactions, and twice the stories to tell. Team victories feel earned and shared — Commander's social magic amplified by partnership.

How the Night Runs
1

Players register two legal decks before the event begins. Both decks must comply with the Two-Headed Commander banned list.

2

Teams are randomly assigned at the start of the event. Neither player knows their partner until the draw — fostering new connections every event.

3

Teammates have 10 minutes to discuss both of their registered decks and choose which one each player will pilot for the night. Once selected, the deck cannot be changed between rounds.

4

Play 3 rounds of Swiss pairings. Teams are matched against randomly selected opponents each round — there is no intentional draw or concession for seeding.

5

At the end of 3 rounds, the team with the best record wins. Tiebreakers are resolved by total game wins, then life total differential at time.

The Forbidden Codex

Banned Cards

These cards are forbidden in Two-Headed Commander. The ban source reflects whether the restriction originates in Commander, Two-Headed Giant, or is unique to this format's cooperative dynamics.

⚖
Ban Philosophy
The principles behind every decision on this list

Magic's diversity of playstyles is a strength — the ban list exists to address genuine imbalance, not to eliminate strategies that some players simply dislike.

The ban list is a living tool, not a permanent verdict — cards can be added or removed as the meta and community feedback evolves.

The goal is to maximise the number of viable decks and encourage a varied meta, while keeping the format competitive and accessible.

Six criteria justify a ban
  1. Cards that force everyone to build around them, homogenizing the meta
  2. Cards whose cost is too low relative to their actual impact
  3. Cards enabling combos too fast or with insufficient mana investment
  4. Cards too oppressive against reasonably or minimally optimized decks
  5. Cards that exploit 2HG's high shared life totals disproportionately
  6. Cards that stall, drag out, or make games unresolvable within one hour — mass land destruction and highly random effects that remove agency are incompatible with the format's structure
⏳
On Extra Turns: A Format Statement
Why every extra turn card is banned — as a category
Why They're Great Cards
✦

In solo Commander, extra turns require real mana investment, reward careful setup, and remain answerable — a legitimate expression of blue's identity.

✦

The design space — protecting a planeswalker, clocking an opponent, resolving a combo — is valid, skillful gameplay in their native formats.

Why They're Banned Here
✖

In Two-Headed Commander, one extra turn is two players untapping simultaneously — the power doubles with no increase in cost.

✖

Turn chains are a degenerate, unfun win pattern — the opposing team sits idle while two players loop turns with no meaningful interaction possible.

The format rewards board-present, decisive wins. Every extra turn card is banned not for individual power level — but because the entire category produces gameplay that undermines what makes Two-Headed Commander worth playing.
